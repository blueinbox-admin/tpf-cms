qa:
  - question: Tell us about your Jewish family background.
    answer: >-
      My mother's side hails from Tashkent, Uzbekistan, with a Persian
      background, and my father comes from Chust, Ukraine. I will do my best to
      depict the history as I understood it. Life wasn’t horrible before leaving
      Russia, but both my mom’s family and my dad’s believed there was more
      opportunity for prosperity and religious freedom if they left. So my dad
      left around the age of ten and my mom around the age of seven.


      In earlier generations, my ancestors were devout and pious Jews, until 4
      or 5 generations ago (depending on which side of the family it was). That
      all changed when the Soviets took power.


      My ancestors struggled in keeping to their Judaism primarily from
      challenges with assimilation. After emigrating from Ukraine, my father was
      put into a big yeshiva in Brooklyn from elementary through high school but
      constantly felt unwelcome or inferior for not speaking the same language
      or being at the level that administrators expected of him at his age. He
      became disillusioned with Judaism and, just like many of his cousins and
      siblings, turned to embrace a more American lifestyle. 


      My mother, on the other hand grew up around family that was assimilated by
      the Soviets. I recall a story from my grandfather’s brother that his bar
      mitzvah was publicized in the paper to attract attention from the
      government so that they would go after his family for practicing Judaism.
  - question: Scary!  What type of Jewish home did you grow up in, and what Jewish
      education did you receive?
    answer: Because of the circumstances described earlier, my mom and dad taught us
      to have pride in our Judaism, without quite understanding what being
      Jewish really meant.  So I grew up riding cars to conservative shul on
      Shabbos and occasionally attending Hebrew school, lighting Chanukah
      candles, and fasting on Yom Kippur but never really connecting or
      understanding what any of those practices really meant.  We bought only
      kosher meat and didn’t mix meat and dairy, but kept a pantry with some
      goods that didn’t have a hechsher.  The values we kept to were all mixed
      up, some American, many Russian, all of which had a Jewish flavor, but
      never quite clear on virtues of the Torah.
