qa:
  - question: Corey, tell us about your Jewish background and ancestry.
    answer: I'm all Ashkenazi; a mix of areas across Eastern Europe, like Poland,
      Lithuania, Ukraine, and Moldova.  My great (x 4) grandfather was Rav
      Efraim Bendet Engleman, the Rav of Kludava (K~~l~~odawa, 1836-1920) and
      his father, the Rav of Koil, who were well-known in central Poland.  Rav
      Efraim Bendet's son, my great-great-great-uncle, Rav Szlomo Engelmen was
      the Rav of Kludava after him.  He was tragically killed in the Holocaust.
  - question: Tragic and fascinating.  Tell us about your Jewish upbringing.  What
      kind of home did you grow up in?
    answer: ⁠I grew up in a Reform Jewish home (if you could call it that).  We only
      did the Jewish thing a few times a year, for major holidays.  I was always
      interested in learning more.  I went to reform camp and went on a reform
      trip to Israel when I was 16.  When I went to Binghamton, I got involved
      in Meor of Binghamton and Chabad on Campus.  I have become more observant
      since starting college.
  - question: How did you find out about The Podcast Fellowship?
    answer: Through Meor Binghamton Rabbi Chaim Harkavy.
  - question: Would you have been interested in The Podcast Fellowship without the
      $15 stipend?
    answer: "Yes, and the proof is that now I learn with Rav Dan Klatzko twice a
      week even though you can only cash out once 😂.  I also have a Chavrusa I
      learn with and I go to kollel once a week with Meor YP in Philly to
      learn.  So bottom line: I love learning and don’t need any incentive to do
      it, but 15 bucks a week is nice I can’t complain. 😂"
  - question: Tell us about your experience with TPF.  What are your interactions
      with your mentor like?  How do you prepare and discuss podcasts with
      him?  Comment on the learning model please.
    answer: Rav Dan and I have a great rapport. We always start out discussing the
      podcast I watched or a topic I’m curious about, but the conversation
      always goes into crazy productive tangents which I love. Just after a few
      months I’m going to need a new notebook, as mine is almost full! 😂
  - question: Who is your favorite speaker/what is your favorite podcast topic?
    answer: ⁠I really like the Parsha with Rav Eisemann ([NOT Your Hebrew
      School](https://podcastfellowship.org/podcasts/courses/torah-portion-of-the-week-on-not-your-hebrew-school))
      because he speaks about it in plain language, in a conversation with one
      of his students.
  - question: Where do you stand in your Jewish observance today?
    answer: ⁠In the past few years I’ve started wrapping tefillin everyday, keeping
      kosher, and learning. Shabbos is a work in progress, but I go to shul most
      Saturday mornings.
  - question: What has been your experience after October 7, 2023?  Have you seen
      antisemitism?  If yes, how do you deal with it?
    answer: Honestly, the administration at Binghamton University was very on top of
      it and squashed a lot of protests and stuff. Sh'koyach to them. Binghamton
      University is very safe; especially with the huge observant Jewish
      community and Jewish community as a whole, I feel like it’ll always be
      safe.
  - question: Can you summarize your experience with The Podcast Fellowship in a
      single quote?
    answer: The Podcast Fellowship not only keeps me on a good schedule to learn
      twice a week, but also inspires me to learn more. Thank you to all who
      make this possible.  I hope more students get inspired to learn this way.
